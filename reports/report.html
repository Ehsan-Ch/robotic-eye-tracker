The perception-to-motion loop

  1. Render a 320 × 240 RGB image from the motor-mounted camera at 25 Hz.
  2. Find red regions, reject small/noisy shapes, and associate candidates with the predicted target bearing.
  3. Use encoder angles to compensate for camera motion. An alpha-beta filter estimates bearing and angular velocity.
  4. Send position targets to two simulated motors, with angle, torque, and command-slew limits.
  5. Predict briefly through missing observations; search and confirm a new detection after longer loss.

This project uses classical vision and feedback control. No neural model, pretrained detector, cloud inference, or learned policy is involved.

What the experiment does not prove

Normal and low-exposure tracking work on this synthetic red-ball task. Fast motion exceeds the controller’s tracking capability. A red distractor can capture its attention because color and motion do not uniquely establish identity.

The experiment uses five reserved seeds, one controller configuration, scripted object trajectories, and simplified motors. It does not establish real-camera performance, general object recognition, or safe operation of physical hardware.
